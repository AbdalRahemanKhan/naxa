What is NAXA?

The complete plain-English explanation. Charts included. Judgemental asides also included.

NAXA is a system that answers one question: "When this big thing happened in the world, what happened to money, markets, and supply chains next?" — automatically, in 90 seconds, with every answer sourced and verifiable.

// honest_origin_story Before NAXA: analyst opens 14 browser tabs, Googles "panama canal stock market", reads 3 paywalled articles, pulls numbers from memory, writes "prices may rise (probably)" and calls it a research note. After NAXA: one API call, structured JSON, every field cited, done before the analyst's coffee cools. The analyst can now use those 18 minutes to contemplate their career choices.

🧒 Explain it like I'm 10

Imagine a detective agency. Something big happens — a huge drought blocks a major shipping canal. The agency immediately opens a case, pulls all the evidence (stock prices, freight costs, commodity prices), digs through old case files to find similar past events, and delivers a complete sourced report with a confidence rating. NAXA is that detective agency. But automated. And it never takes lunch breaks.

Alternative data API mapping downstream signal chains from macro events. Returns structured JSON with backtested confidence scores (n=4→12), cited sources, and lag-calibrated directional signals across freight rates, equities, and commodities. Infrastructure for the agent economy. Wedge: shipping vertical.

🔑 The golden rule

Scripts 1–3 measure the present event. Scripts 4–5 compare it to the past. Confidence comes from pattern frequency across history — never from an AI's opinion. The LLM reads data and describes it. That's it. It is not allowed to have feelings about the confidence score.

// 05 — confidence scores

The Confidence Score

What 0.71 actually means. Why it's not just made up. Why it caps at 0.85.

// why_this_matters Most "AI confidence scores" are vibes laundered through a softmax function. NAXA's scores are: hit rate across historical events, weighted by data quality, penalized for small samples. A score of 0.71 means "in 71% of comparable historical cases, this pattern held — accounting for data quality and our current sample size of 4." That's a sentence you can say in a meeting without being laughed out of the room.

raw_score = (hit_rate × 0.6) + (consistency × 0.4)
↓ apply sample size penalty
final = 0.5 + (raw_score − 0.5) × (1 − 1/√n)
capped at 0.85 until n ≥ 12

🔑 Why it caps at 0.85

With n=4, even a 100% hit rate doesn't mean we're fully confident. We might have just gotten lucky 4 times. The cap enforces intellectual honesty: no score above 0.85 until we have 12+ historical events. A quant who tells you they're 95% confident based on 4 data points is either lying or selling something.

// 07 — the scripts

Phase 1 Scripts — Explained

What each file does. One job per script. No exceptions.

pull_shipping_data.py
Downloads stock prices for 6 shipping companies via Yahoo Finance. Normalizes to 100 at baseline. Calculates % move at 14/30/60 days from event. Saves to data/raw/. Rule: raw data is sacred — never overwrite.
pull_fred_data.py
Calls the Federal Reserve's free API for 5 economic series (freight PPI, oil, gas, corn, soybean). Handles the daily vs monthly frequency problem. Everything has a citable FRED series ID — that's what makes it auditable.
build_master_timeline.py
Combines Day 1 + Day 2 data into one unified signal chain table. Produces the 4-panel chart showing all layers together. This is the visual proof that the chain exists and has measurable lag structure.
historical_events_db.py
The memory bank. 4 comparable historical disruptions, each with sourced % moves per signal layer. Every entry has data quality flags (PRIMARY/PARTIAL/ESTIMATED) that weight the confidence calculation. This is the n= foundation.
calculate_confidence.py
Event study engine. Reads the historical database. Calculates: weighted hit rate, magnitude consistency, sample size penalty. Outputs structured confidence objects with full methodology. No LLM involvement at any point.
build_gold_standard.py
Assembles the final JSON from all previous outputs. Every field sourced. Schema v0.1.0 is locked — Phase 2 must produce this exact structure automatically.
validate_gold_standard.py
30+ checks on the JSON before it leaves the system. Confidence scores in range. Sources present. No overconfident language. Fail fast: better to crash internally than serve broken data to a customer.
